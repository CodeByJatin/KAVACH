# NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Standards Compendium (v2024-Q3)

## 1. Approved PQC Algorithms (FIPS Standards)
- **FIPS 203: ML-KEM (Module-Lattice-Based Key-Encapsulation Mechanism)**
  - Primary mechanism for secure key exchange over public channels.
  - Replaces RSA, ECDH, and X25519.
  - Required for all TLS 1.3 handshakes moving forward (Hybrid Kyber integration recommended during transition).

- **FIPS 204: ML-DSA (Module-Lattice-Based Digital Signature Algorithm)**
  - Primary standard for digital signatures and identity verification.
  - Replaces RSA signatures and ECDSA.

- **FIPS 205: SLH-DSA (Stateless Hash-Based Digital Signature Algorithm)**
  - Backup/alternative mechanism for digital signatures.
  - Offers extreme long-term security properties at the cost of processing speed/signature size.

## 2. Deprecated Classical Algorithms (Shor's Algorithm Vulnerable)
The following protocols are mathematically vulnerable to Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computers (CRQC) and must be phased out:
- **RSA (Rivest-Shamir-Adleman):** All key sizes (1024, 2048, 4096) are fundamentally compromised.
- **ECC (Elliptic-Curve Cryptography):** ECDH and ECDSA are fully compromised.
- **Diffie-Hellman (DH/DHE):** Finite-field key exchanges are compromised.

## 3. Migration and Compliance Roadmaps (CNSA 2.0)
- **Hybrid Cryptography:** Organizations must immediately adopt Hybrid TLS (e.g., `X25519Kyber768Draft00`) to maintain classical compliance while establishing quantum resistance against "Store Now, Decrypt Later" (SNDL) attacks.
- **Symmetric Keys:** AES-256 and SHA-384/512 remain quantum-safe due to Grover's algorithm halving effective key space. AES-128 is considered insufficient for long-term classification.
